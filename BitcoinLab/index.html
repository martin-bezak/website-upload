    Three
    strategies

    Same signals, three levels of risk. All of them were tested hour by hour since 2018 with trading fees, and all earn interest on the money that is not in Bitcoin. Full rules and numbers.

    Growth

    Stays fully invested in Bitcoin and only steps aside for one specific danger: a calm stretch that ends with a break downwards. Historically, those breaks came before some of Bitcoin’s fastest falls.

    How it decides

    • Default: 100% Bitcoin.
    • Sells everything when Bitcoin has been unusually quiet (volatility in the lowest 20% of the past year) and then a daily close falls below its 20-day range.
    • Buys back when that move is over: the price climbs back to the top of the range (stop), falls twice as far as that risk (target), or 20 days pass.

    What to expect

    For: long-term holders who want the highest return and can sit through deep drops; it still held through most of 2022.

    Balanced

    Owns Bitcoin only while it is in a clear uptrend and waits in cash, earning interest, the rest of the time. The calm-then-break signal can move it in or out early, before the trend confirms.

    How it decides

    • Buys (100%) when a daily close is above the 50-, 100- and 200-day averages.
    • Sells (to cash) when a close falls below any of them.
    • Early moves: after a quiet stretch, a close above the 20-day range buys and a close below it sells, until that trade’s stop, target or 20 days.

    What to expect

    For: beating buy-and-hold with about half its worst drop, if you accept missing the first part of big rallies.

    Defensive

    Makes the same buy and sell decisions as Balanced, but holds less Bitcoin when Bitcoin is wild, so a bad week hurts less. When markets are calm it holds the full amount.

    How it decides

    • When to own Bitcoin: exactly as Balanced.
    • How much: 50% ÷ Bitcoin’s current yearly volatility, at most 100%. At 80% volatility it holds 62%; at 40% or less, everything.
    • Small changes (under 10 points) are skipped to save fees.

    What to expect

    For: the steadiest ride: the smallest drops of the three, still ahead of buy-and-hold since 2018.
