How it
works

Three strategies, built from two signals that survived a long series of tests. This page gives the exact rules, the numbers, and the ways they can go wrong. The Archive tells how we got here, including everything that failed.

The two signals

1 · Trend

Bitcoin is “in an uptrend” when its daily closing price is above its average closing price of the last 50, 100 and 200 days. If it is below any of them, the trend is off.

Why it can work: Bitcoin’s big moves last months, and its crashes rarely happen while the price is above all three averages. Staying out when the trend is off skipped most of 2018 and 2022. The price of this: entering and leaving late, and some false starts in sideways markets.

2 · Squeeze breakout

  1. A calm stretch. Bitcoin’s volatility over the last 20 days is in the lowest 20% of the past year. This “arms” the signal for 10 days.
  2. The range. The highest high and lowest low of the last 20 days.
  3. The break. A daily close below the range is a breakdown (sell); above it, a breakout (buy).
  4. The exit. The trade ends when the price reaches the other side of the range (the stop), moves twice as far as the risk in the trade’s direction (the target, “2R”), or after 20 days, whichever comes first. Stops and targets are checked against every hour’s high and low.

Why it can work: unusually quiet markets tend to be followed by big moves, and Bitcoin’s calm-then-crash pattern repeated in Nov 2018, Feb–Mar 2020, Jan and May 2022 and Nov 2025. The breakdown side passed every test (it beat random timing in 995 of 1,000 simulations); the breakout side is weaker evidence.

Growth

  1. Hold 100% Bitcoin.
  2. On a squeeze breakdown (daily close below the range), sell everything right after that close.
  3. Buy everything back when that trade ends: stop above, target below, or 20 days.
  4. While in cash, earn the T-bill rate.

When it does badly: long falls that start without a calm stretch first (it held through most of 2022’s −64%, losing −46%), and breakdowns that reverse straight away (July 2021: sold, then Bitcoin rose 19% before the stop bought back).

Balanced

  1. Each day at the close (00:00 UTC): if the trend is on, hold 100% Bitcoin; if it is off, hold 0% and earn interest.
  2. A squeeze breakdown sells to 0% even in an uptrend; a squeeze breakout buys 100% even before the trend turns. Each override lasts until its trade ends (stop, 2R target or 20 days); then the trend rule takes over again.

When it does badly: sideways years, when the price keeps crossing its averages (2025 roughly broke even while Bitcoin lost 6%), and sharp recoveries it joins late (2023: +95% against Bitcoin’s +156%).

Defensive

  1. The same decisions as Balanced.
  2. But when in Bitcoin, hold only min(100%, 50% ÷ Bitcoin’s current yearly volatility). Volatility is measured the RiskMetrics way (an exponentially weighted average of daily moves, λ = 0.94). At 80% volatility that means 62% Bitcoin; at 40% or less, 100%.
  3. Position changes under 10 percentage points are skipped to save fees.

When it does badly: the same as Balanced, plus it holds less during the wildest rallies (2020: +132% against Balanced’s +165%).

Interest on cash

All three strategies spend time out of Bitcoin (Balanced and Defensive about 60% of the time). That money earns the 3-month US Treasury bill rate of each day: almost 0% in 2020–21, about 5% in 2023–24, – now. It added about 2% a year to Balanced from 2020 on, without any extra market risk. A version with only half that rate still came out ahead.

In practice this means T-bills, a money market fund, or a stablecoin savings product. They don’t carry the same risk: T-bills are backed by the US government, stablecoin products by the company offering them.

How it was tested

Data
Hourly Bitcoin prices on Binance since August 2017 (Binance Vision). Every decision uses only data that existed at that moment.
Execution
Bitcoin trades around the clock, but every rule reads daily closes (00:00 UTC), so a signal can only be known when the day ends. The backtest trades at the first price after that close. Stops and targets are checked against every hour’s high and low; if both are touched in the same hour, the stop is assumed to come first. A stop that the price gaps through fills at the worse price.
Costs
0.10% fee plus 0.05% slippage on every trade. Re-tested at two and three times the costs, with 0.5% worse stop fills: all three strategies still beat buy-and-hold.
Trading later
This site updates around 03:30 UTC, hours after the close the backtest trades at. So every trade was re-run 1 to 12 hours late (stops and targets unchanged, since they are orders placed with the trade). Returns barely moved: Growth 44× to 58×, Balanced 31× to 34×, Defensive 20× to 22×, all far ahead of Bitcoin’s 12×. The worst drop moved more: Balanced −44% to −49%, Defensive −28% to −34%. At about 4 hours late, roughly when you can act on this page: 52×, 31× and 20×, with worst drops of −61%, −49% and −33%.
Rules first
Every rule was written down before its first test, with standard values, not tuned to the results. The main hypotheses were registered in advance and corrected for testing several ideas at once (Holm method); only the squeeze breakdown passed (p = 0.010).
Placebos
The same exits from 1,000 sets of random entry times, and the same positions shifted to random dates. A real signal has to beat both.
No hindsight tuning
A version that picked the best of 32 rule variants each January, using only past data, did worse out of sample than the fixed rules ($8.2 against $16.4 per $1 from 2020). So the rules stay fixed.

Risks and limits

  • History is short. About two and a half Bitcoin market cycles. The evidence that the strategies beat buy-and-hold on risk-adjusted terms is borderline for Balanced and Defensive (p ≈ 0.05–0.10); it is strong only for the squeeze breakdown.
  • The ideas came after looking. The rules were fixed before each test, but the ideas themselves were chosen with knowledge of Bitcoin’s past. The live record, starting October 2026, is the real test.
  • Big drops still happen. Growth fell 61% at its worst; Balanced 42%; Defensive 27%. Trading a few hours after the close, as you will with this page, made the worst drops of Balanced and Defensive up to 7 points deeper in tests (trading later).
  • Long dull stretches. Trend strategies can trail Bitcoin for a year or more in fast rallies.
  • One exchange. Prices are Binance’s; other exchanges differ slightly, and exchanges can fail.
  • Taxes are not included. Every sell can be a taxable event where you live.

Following it in practice

Bitcoin trades all day, but the rules only look at the daily close (00:00 UTC). The page updates once a night, around 03:30 UTC, when the day’s data is published; trade when you see the new action. Stop and target levels can be set as orders on the exchange straight away. The Strategy page shows that night’s action, the levels that would change it, and any open trade’s stop and target. Following a strategy means checking once a day and trading on a spot exchange (no leverage, no shorting), plus holding the cash part somewhere that earns interest.

This is a research project, not investment advice. The rules don’t know your goals, taxes or tolerance for losses, and past results don’t guarantee future ones.

Words used here

Moving average
The average closing price of the last N days.
Volatility
How much the price moves around, as a yearly percentage. Bitcoin is usually at 50–80%; the S&P 500 at about 15–20%.
Worst drop
The biggest fall from a peak to a later low (maximum drawdown).
Sharpe ratio
Average return divided by volatility, per year. A way to compare return for the same amount of risk.
R, 2R
R is the risk in a trade: the distance from entry to stop. A 2R target is twice that distance in the trade’s favour.
p-value
How likely a result at least this good would be from luck alone. Below 0.05 is the usual bar.
